Ludus

Take a little break

A small panel of games that lives in your menu bar or system tray. Click the controller, play a round of Snake, Sudoku or a quick duel against the computer, then get back to work. Your place is saved when you close it.

Download for MacmacOS 14 or later, Apple silicon Download for WindowsWindows 10 or 11, 64-bit

Mac: macOS 14 Sonoma or later on Apple silicon (M1 or newer). Windows: Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit. Both are free and open source. Everything works offline except Play online and Map Hunt.

What it is

A break you can close in one click

Ludus sits next to the clock. It opens as a small panel over whatever you are doing and closes the moment you click elsewhere. No windows to manage, no accounts, no ads.

Picks up where you left off

Close the panel mid-game and it pauses. Open it later and the game is still there, along with your best scores.

Rounds that fit a short break

Every game is built to be played in a minute or two, with keyboard controls and mouse or trackpad where it makes sense.

Private and offline

Everything runs on your computer. Ludus only goes online if you choose Play online or Map Hunt, and collects nothing.

New in V2

Play online with up to four friends

Make a room, share the code and play together on Mac and Windows. No accounts, nothing to sign up for.

A room for up to four

Create a room, then share its 5 character code or the invite link. Friends type the code or click the link and they are in.

Single game or Tournament

Play one game, or line up several in a Tournament. Every game you win is worth 1 point, and the scoreboard shows who is ahead.

Rejoin with the same code

Dropped out or closed the panel by mistake? Rejoin brings you back into the same room, with the same code.

Works with older versions

Friends on an older Ludus can still join your room. They play one game at a time, and everyone else can enjoy the new modes.

Smooth for the person joining

Paddle Duel, Air Hockey, Tag, City Swallow, Land Claim and Worm Arena feel quick even on a slow connection, because the joiner's own moves show up at once.

Mac and Windows together

A Mac player and a Windows player can sit in the same room. Rooms are not private, so only share the code with people you trust.

New game

Map Hunt: where in the world is this?

A real street-level photo from somewhere on Earth. Look around, work out where you are and drop a pin on the map. Needs an internet connection.

Look around and move

Turn the view to read the signs, the road and the trees, and move along the road to see more.

A timer and three modes

Play with a timer if you like. Choose Move, No move, or NMPZ, where you cannot move, pan or zoom.

Five rounds

Each game has five rounds. The closer your pin, the more points you get.

Online for 2 to 4 players

Play it in an online room and everyone gets the same places, so you can compare guesses.

New covers and Rock Paper Scissors

New artwork for City Swallow, Land Claim, Crate Push and Worm Arena, and a new Rock Paper Scissors, solo or against a friend online with secret picks.

Share with a friend

On a Mac, one click packs the app up and opens the share sheet, so you can send it to a friend by AirDrop.

Install

Two minutes, then it lives by the clock

Ludus is not sold through an app store, so your computer asks you to confirm the first time you open it. That is expected; the steps below show where to click.

Mac

macOS 14 Sonoma or later, Apple silicon (M1 or newer)

  1. Download Ludus.dmg and open it.
  2. Drag Ludus into Applications, then open it from there.
  3. If macOS asks the first time: in Applications, right-click Ludus and choose Open, then Open again. On newer macOS versions, if you see no Open button, go to System Settings, Privacy & Security, and click Open Anyway.
  4. Look for the controller icon in the menu bar and click it.

Why the extra step: builds that are not notarised by Apple need you to confirm once. A notarised build opens straight away. Homebrew install is coming soon.

Windows

Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit

  1. Download Ludus-windows.zip, right-click it and choose Extract All. Keep the Ludus folder somewhere permanent, for example Documents.
  2. Open the folder and run Ludus.exe.
  3. If Windows shows "Windows protected your PC", click More info, then Run anyway.
  4. If Windows asks for the .NET Desktop Runtime (or WebView2 on some Windows 10 PCs), accept the free Microsoft download, then run Ludus.exe again.
  5. Click the controller icon in the system tray. If you cannot see it, click the small arrow next to the tray icons.

Why the warning: this build is not code signed yet, so SmartScreen does not recognise it.

Questions

Good to know

Does it work offline?

Yes, for almost everything. Every game and every sound is inside the app. Ludus only uses the internet for Play online, and for Map Hunt, which loads street photos and map pictures.

Where are my scores and saved games kept?

On your own computer only. On a Mac they are kept in the app's preferences; on Windows in a file at %APPDATA%\Ludus\state.json. Closing the panel or quitting keeps your place.

What does Ludus collect about me?

Nothing. There are no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no tracking of any kind, and nothing you do in Ludus leaves your computer.

How do I open it when my computer starts?

Turn on "Open at login" in Ludus settings (the gear icon), or on Windows right-click the tray icon.

What keys do the games use?

Arrow keys or WASD for most games, Space to act, Esc or P to pause. Each game shows its own controls under the play area. Two-player games share one keyboard.

How do I remove it?

Turn off "Open at login" and quit Ludus from its settings. On a Mac, drag Ludus from Applications to the Bin. On Windows, delete the Ludus folder, and %APPDATA%\Ludus if you also want to remove your scores.

Can I read the code?

Yes. Ludus is open source under the MIT licence: read it, build it, change it, share it. It is on GitHub.

Unsigned app: how to open it

This app is not signed yet (Apple's developer account is paid, and one is not set up yet), so your computer warns you the first time. Mac: right-click the app, choose Open, then Open again (or System Settings > Privacy & Security > Open Anyway). Windows: in SmartScreen click More info, then Run anyway. You can check your download against the SHA-256 fingerprints listed in the project's CHECKSUMS.md on GitHub.
